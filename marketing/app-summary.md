## What it is, in one sentence

The Balancing Act is a personal assistant that pulls your calendar, email, to-dos, and reminders into one place. You tell it what's on your mind in plain words, and it keeps track for you, so there's a little less on your mind.

## The problem it solves

People juggling work, home, family, and their own lives carry a mental load of appointments, school emails, errands, and things they remembered in the grocery aisle. That information is scattered across calendars, inboxes, texts, and memory. The Balancing Act brings it together, so you don't have to remember it all.

## Who it's for

The demo's copy is written for busy people who wear several hats, such as work, home, family, and personal. Parents are a natural fit, since the examples include school pickup and a school field-trip waiver. Suggested audiences to test:
- Busy parents managing a family calendar and school communications
- Working professionals juggling meetings with personal errands
- Anyone who feels they're "the one who remembers everything" at home

(This audience is inferred from the demo's content. It is not based on research results.)

## How it works (what someone can do in the demo)

1. **Say hello.** A short welcome asks for a first name, an optional phone number, and a time zone. Visitors can also skip straight to the demo.
2. **Talk to the personal assistant.** This is the home screen, a chat where you type what's on your mind in everyday language:
   - "Put my dentist appointment tomorrow at 4pm in the schedule"
   - "Meeting with Sarah at 3," "Team standup Monday 9am," "Lunch with Amy 11 to 1 tomorrow"
   - "Remind me to call mom Friday at 5pm," "Remind me to pay rent on 12/1"
   - "What does my day look like?"
3. **Approve before anything happens.** The assistant proposes each appointment or reminder, and you approve it. You stay in control.
4. **See your next 24 hours.** One gentle look ahead shows your plans and to-dos together in a timeline, with later meetings listed under "Coming up later."
5. **Get a daily brief.** Choose one or more times a day and a format: everything in order, grouped by the hats you wear (work, home, family, you), or just the essentials. The brief is designed to arrive as a text message.
6. **Get help with email.** Your inbox is sorted into "Needs attention," "All mail," and "Promotions." You can check off an email, turn it into a to-do, or move it to Trash.
7. **Connect accounts.** The planned connections are Google email and Google Calendar, with Microsoft, Apple/iCloud, and a dedicated number you can text your assistant listed as coming later.

## Key benefits to emphasize

- **Less remembering, more being here.** Offload appointments, reminders, and to-dos to an assistant.
- **Just talk to it.** No forms or fiddly calendar screens. Type it the way you'd say it.
- **Everything in one place.** Calendar, email, to-dos, and reminders together.
- **You have the final say.** It asks before it changes anything.
- **A brief that fits your life.** A heads-up at the times you choose, in the format you like.

## Important truths for honest ads

- The app is **in development and not launched**. The website is an **interactive demo with sample data**. It does not connect to real accounts, and it doesn't send real reminders or texts yet.
- Don't claim it reduces stress, saves a specific amount of time, uses a particular AI model, or has users or reviews. We haven't measured any of that.

## Visual identity

- **Name styling:** written in lowercase, "the balancing act", in a serif font, with the small label "PERSONAL ASSISTANT" or "YOUR PERSONAL ASSISTANT" underneath.
- **Logo:** a soft pale-yellow four-pointed sparkle (✳-like star) on a deep green rounded square.
- **Colors:**
  - Deep forest green `#254f46` (primary, buttons, logo background, welcome panel)
  - Dark green-gray text `#293e37`
  - Warm off-white paper `#f7f7f2` (backgrounds)
  - Soft lime cream `#e7eac8` and pale yellow `#e5e8b7` (accents, logo sparkle)
  - Muted sage gray `#7b857c` (secondary text)
  - Warm tan `#c39260` (small highlight accent)
- **Fonts:** Georgia-style serif for headlines (elegant and editorial). DM Sans for body text (clean and friendly).
- **Look and feel:** calm and airy, with lots of white space, rounded cards, soft borders, and small symbols (✳ sparkle, ☀ sun, ✉ envelope, ♡ heart). It should feel like a deep breath, not a busy dashboard.
- **Imagery ideas:** a phone showing a friendly text message brief; a hand-written-feeling to-do list getting lighter; a calm parent with coffee while the day "sorts itself"; floating chips of everyday tasks being checked off.
